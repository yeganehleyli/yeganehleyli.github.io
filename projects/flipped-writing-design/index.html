Writing with intention

Integrating Self-Regulated Learning in Flipped Learning to Enhance Argumentative Writing with Digital Tools

A closer look at how digital activities supported goal-setting, progress monitoring, and reflection in a 16-week argumentative-writing study.

Expanded companion case study to Learning to take the lead, with additional design and evaluation detail from the same study.

Tools & methods

Google Docs, EdPuzzle, Padlet, Quizizz, essay assessment, a 41-item questionnaire, and baseline-adjusted analysis.

The instructional question

A flipped classroom provides time for practice, but learners also need strategies for planning, monitoring progress, and reflecting on their work. As first and corresponding author, I collaborated on a study asking whether explicit self-regulated learning support could strengthen argumentative writing beyond flipped learning alone.

The 16-week study involved 240 medical students with intermediate English proficiency at the University of Kashan in Fall 2023.

Connect each tool to a learning purpose

We compared flipped instruction with and without explicit self-regulated learning support. The digital tools were aligned with specific parts of the learning process.

Instructional tool choices
ToolLearning purpose
Google DocsGoal-setting and collaborative drafting
EdPuzzlePre-class comprehension checks
PadletStructured reflection
QuizizzPractice through gamified quizzes

We assessed pre- and post-intervention essays for claims, qualifiers, evidence, and coherence, alongside a 41-item self-regulated learning questionnaire. Two raters evaluated essays without knowing group assignments. The analysis compared writing outcomes while accounting for baseline performance and examined changes in self-reported learning skills.

Within the supported group, mean claim scores increased from 1.52 to 2.75 and qualifier scores from 1.46 to 3.19. Qualifiers help writers limit or specify an argument rather than frame it as universally true.

Approximately 15% of experimental-group essays still lacked appropriate qualifiers, identifying a continuing need for targeted instruction.

How to read the evidence

The writing insights above interpret assessed outcomes; the paper did not report a separate qualitative interview or thematic analysis. The study resulted in a first-author 2026 publication in the Journal of Modern Research in English Language Studies.

What I learned

Effective digital instruction depends on connecting tools to clear learning processes. Explicit goals, progress monitoring, and reflection helped organize the writing experience, while remaining difficulties with qualifiers pointed to a need for more focused support.

In future research, I would combine performance measures with interviews and systematic analysis of reflective journals, then examine whether improvements persist over time.
