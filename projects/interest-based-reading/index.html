Reading through what matters

Using Generative AI to Support Reading Engagement in an Adolescent with Reading Disabilities

Connecting students’ interests with the reading demands of mathematics through adapted materials, varied activities, and ongoing tutoring support.

My contribution
Mathematics tutor and instructional-material designer
Participants
Adolescent learner with reading disabilities
Project context
Windsor High School tutoring under Binghamton University supervision

In this case study

The learning challengeHow I shaped the learning materialsWhat I observedWhat I learned

Tools & methods

Generative AI, interest-based stories, games, drawing, puzzles, tutoring observations, and biweekly team reflection.

The learning challenge

Mathematical problem-solving often depends on reading a written prompt, following its meaning, and feeling willing to try. During spring and fall 2025, I worked as a mathematics tutor at Windsor High School under Binghamton University supervision, within an NSF-supported team project.

My responsibility was to design personalized mathematics activities that incorporated students’ interests while supporting the reading demands of problem-solving. This case focuses on reading engagement for an adolescent with reading disabilities.

How I shaped the learning materials

  1. Begin with meaningful interests

    I used generative AI to help develop interest-based materials, then adapted them to the learner’s needs. Storytelling connected written mathematics problems with contexts that could make participation more appealing.

  2. Offer several ways to participate

    Games, drawing, and puzzles provided varied ways to engage with written problems. The materials supported tutoring and interaction, with my instructional judgment guiding their use.

  3. Observe, discuss, and refine

    I observed reading accuracy, fluency, participation, and willingness to attempt problem-solving. In biweekly meetings, teammates and our supervisor discussed progress, exchanged strategies, and helped refine the approach.

What I observed

  • Reading

    Tutoring observations indicated improvements in reading accuracy and fluency while working with the adapted materials.

  • Participation

    The learner showed stronger engagement with personalized materials and greater motivation to attempt mathematical problem-solving.

  • Instructional connection

    Linking literacy demands with interests offered a practical way to make mathematics tasks more approachable within tutoring.

How to read the evidence

These findings are based on observations and qualitative analysis. A named reading assessment and verified baseline/final scores were not available in the project summary, so no numerical improvement is claimed here. The evidence does not isolate generative AI’s contribution from personalization, tutoring, or the other instructional strategies.

What I learned

Interest-based instruction can create useful connections between literacy and mathematics. Combining personalized materials, varied activities, observation, and collaborative reflection helped me respond to the learner throughout tutoring.

This project also reinforced the need for clear measurement. In future work, I would track reading accuracy, fluency, engagement, and problem-solving motivation separately, with documented baseline and final measures.
